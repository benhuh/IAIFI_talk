Expanding the Horizons of Generalization

Integrating two distinct generalization paradigms:

Smooth Manifold Learning + Exact Algebraic Axioms
→ Toward natively discovering the discrete, symbolic laws of nature.

Step 0 (0:00 - 0:45) - Column 1: The Status Quo

  • Natural data resides on a very low-dimensional manifold compared to its ambient dimensions.
  • Deep neural networks generalize by being very good at exploiting this geometric compressibility—by discovering this intrinsic low-dimensional manifold and smoothly interpolating over it.
  • This mechanism has been rigorously studied in the canonical setting of matrix factorization on the classic matrix completion task, revealing exactly how deep architectures build a natural preference toward low-rank solutions.
  • This implicit low-rank bias reveals the Occam's Razor of deep learning—the notion of simplicity innate to the model.

Step 1 (0:45 - 1:30) - Column 2: The New Paradigm

  • While this geometric low-rank bias is highly effective for the continuous, in-distribution approximation of natural data, it has a fundamental blind spot. It is prone to learning spurious features and struggles to efficiently discover exact, algorithmic rules that robustly generalize out-of-distribution.
  • The canonical testbed for this (effect) is recovering a finite binary operation table, as shown here, which describes $x \circ y = z$, for an unknown operation $\circ$—essentially the symbolic, and algebraic counterpart to matrix completion.
  • Standard deep learning models notoriously suffer at this task. That's because "rank" is not the right Occam's Razor for algebraic structures.
  • The true algorithmic simplicity is measured by the Minimum Description Length, or MDL. And for this task, MDL is largely determined by Associativity of the operation, which is a fundamentally distinct notion of simplicity.
  • How can we induce such simplicity bias?

Step 2 (1:30 - 2:30) - Column 2: Associativity & Flatness

  • First, we use a tensor factorization model that maps symbols to matrix embeddings, rather than standard vector embeddings.
  • Then, we optimize for a flatness by minimizing the Hessian trace of the landscape.
  • Remarkably, we prove that this trace yields an exact, differentiable metric for associativity (violations) (up to isotopy) by enforcing homomorphism of the matrix embeddings.
  • This proves that the discrete algebraic notion of simplicity can be natively induced within the continuous, differentiable framework of deep learning.

Step 3 (2:30 - 5:00) - Column 3: Results & Counter-Example

  • The numerical results confirm the theory. Our model natively discovers the exact group structures and the factor tensors converge to the exact unitary representation of the group.
  • Moreover, this model acts as a highly efficient compressor, significantly outperforming standard gzip.
  • For an $S_4$ isotope—essentially a disguised, permuted version of the group—standard gzip gets stuck at 366 bytes. Our method compresses it down to 76 bytes, operating much closer to the theoretical MDL limit.
  • This works because it genuinely identifies the exact algebraic laws underlying the table, rather than just exploiting surface-level patterns.
  • While not shown in this slide, our broader empirical results show this native structural bias also yields massive improvements in convergence speed and sample efficiency over standard models.
  • Conclusion: This work provides an existence proof for a fundamentally distinct paradigm, demonstrating that generalization can be driven by discovering exact algebraic axioms rather than learning smooth manifolds for interpolation.
  • Ultimately, it points toward a future where neural networks don't just perform continuous approximation, but also natively discover the discrete, symbolic laws of nature—such as extracting unknown symmetry groups directly from raw data. Thank you.
